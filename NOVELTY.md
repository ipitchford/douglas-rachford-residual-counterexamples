# Contribution and literature boundary

This paper addresses Conjecture 3.5 of Abbaszadehpeivasti and Zamani, arXiv:2509.06676v2. The source statements and inspected versions are recorded in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## Contribution map

| Result | Contribution boundary |
|---|---|
| Exact scalar and planar counterexamples | Refutations of the printed conjecture under its stated assumptions. No earlier direct refutation was located in the bounded search below. |
| Sharp worst-case exponent for fixed admissible parameters | A class-specific conclusion combining a standard averaged-map upper bound with a horizon-dependent cocoercive rotation family. The full mixed-class sharp constant is not determined. |
| Prescribed-schedule linear envelope | An explicit corollary of the classical contraction polynomial inequality and planar boundary constructions, not a newly discovered polynomial principle. |
| Adaptive scalar-policy minimax and penalty | A synthesis of the established sharp proximal-point bound and published rotation-product algebra, with exact adaptive/pathwise quantifiers and a penalty identity. Historical priority for this formulation is not asserted. |
| Corollary 6.1: conventional-range linear policies | Elementary consequence of the linear envelope and rotation factors: an exact adaptive minimax infimum with nonattainment in the open interval. No separate firstness claim; no corresponding nonlinear restricted-policy theorem is asserted. |
| Hinge examples and analytic perturbation family | Exact finite-horizon examples beyond the elementary scalar and pure-rotation envelopes considered in this paper. No minimal failure horizon or full mixed-class envelope is claimed. |

The paper does not establish an impossibility result for all accelerated algorithms: anchoring, extra directions, matrix updates, and alternative output rules lie outside its scalar-policy class. Its rotation lower bounds choose an instance for each horizon; individual examples can converge geometrically. The originating paper's objective-value conjectures remain unresolved here.
